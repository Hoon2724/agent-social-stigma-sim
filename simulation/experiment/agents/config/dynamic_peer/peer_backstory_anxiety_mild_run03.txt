There were times I’d sit at my desk, staring at the same sentence for hours, trying to make it sound just right — like if I got it wrong, something would be revealed about me that I couldn’t bear to see. For a long time, I handled it all by myself, because asking for help felt like admitting there was something wrong with me. I was terrified that if I told someone, they’d think I was broken or weak, that I’d be seen as less than who I was. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, not sure what I expected but certain I needed to say something out loud. The appointment was quiet, just me and the person listening, and what I found out was that I wasn’t broken — I was just trying to survive something I didn’t know how to name. Now, I still feel the pressure sometimes, but I know it doesn’t mean I’m flawed. It just means I’m still learning how to let myself be seen.
