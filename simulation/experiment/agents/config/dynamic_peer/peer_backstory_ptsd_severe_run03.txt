I used to be the kind of person who would just get up and keep going, even when my body had already said no. There were times when I’d feel myself flinch before I even registered what had happened — a sudden noise, a movement out of the corner of my eye — and I’d be on the floor before I could process it. For a long time, I didn’t tell anyone. I didn’t want to be seen as someone who couldn’t handle things, someone who needed help. When I finally thought about talking to someone, I worried they’d see me as broken or weak, like there was something fundamentally wrong with me. I went to a counsellor, sat in a small room, and said the things I’d been holding back for years. What they saw wasn’t a person who was broken — they saw someone who had been trying their best in a world that didn’t always make sense. Now, I still feel that sudden drop sometimes, but I don’t let it define me. I know I’m not the only one, and I know it doesn’t mean I’m less than who I am.
