There was a time when I would stay up late, replaying conversations in my head, wondering if I’d said the wrong thing or if I’d been too quiet. I handled it alone for a while because asking for help felt like admitting something was wrong with me. I was scared that if I went to a counsellor, they’d see me as broken or weak. But eventually, I went — not because I had to, but because I wanted to know if there was another way to feel. The appointment was quiet, mostly just sitting there while someone listened. What it turned out to be was that I wasn’t the problem — just that I’d been carrying something I didn’t know how to put down. Now, I still feel things deeply, but I don’t let them define me the way I used to.
