There were times when I'd feel my breath catch or my pulse quicken in situations that should have been routine. For a while, I handled it alone — telling myself that if I asked for help, it would mean I was broken or weak. I worried that seeing a professional would confirm that I was the kind of person who couldn't handle things on my own, that there was something fundamentally wrong with me. Eventually, I went to a counsellor. The appointment wasn't dramatic — just a quiet room, a chair, and a conversation that didn't make me feel judged or misunderstood. What it turned out to be was simply this: that I wasn't alone in feeling this way, and that asking for help didn't mean I had failed. I still have days when it feels like I'm walking on thin ice, but I know now that needing support doesn't define who I am — it's just part of the journey.
