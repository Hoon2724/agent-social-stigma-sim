I remember the way my body moved before my brain could catch up — like a reflex, like something had already decided for me. For a long time, I kept it to myself, because I was afraid that if I said anything, it would mean I was broken or weak or not okay. I worried that if I went to someone, they’d see something wrong with me, like I was defective or damaged in a way I couldn’t fix. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just once, and sat there in the silence while they listened. What they said wasn’t what I expected — they didn’t say I was broken. They said it was normal, and that it didn’t mean anything bad about me. I still feel it sometimes, that old rush of panic before I can stop it — but now I know it doesn’t define me, and I don’t have to hide it anymore.
