I remember the way my body moved before my mind could catch up — like my feet had already decided to run, or drop, or freeze, long before I knew what was happening. For a while, I handled it all on my own, because asking for help felt like admitting I was broken somehow. I was scared that if I talked to someone, they’d see me as weak, or unstable, or not someone worth listening to. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just like I’d told myself I never would. The appointment was quiet, not loud or dramatic — just me sitting there, talking, and the person listening without judgment. What I found out was that it wasn’t about being broken, or wrong, or anything like that. It was just my body’s way of trying to keep me safe. I still don’t have all the answers, but I don’t let the fear of needing help define me anymore.
