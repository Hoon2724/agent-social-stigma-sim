I used to feel my body move before my mind could catch up — like when I’d be walking somewhere and suddenly my legs would go numb, or I’d freeze in place, as if I’d forgotten how to move. For a long time, I didn’t tell anyone. I thought that if I asked, it would mean I was broken or weak, that there was something fundamentally wrong with me. I worried that if I saw a professional, they’d look at me and say, "You’re not okay," like I’d failed at being human. Eventually, I went to a therapist. The appointment was quiet, and I didn’t say much. But after that, I started to see that what I was feeling wasn’t a sign of being broken — it was just my body trying to keep me safe. Now, I still have moments where I feel that same sudden jolt, but I don’t run from it anymore. I know it doesn’t define me — just something I have to keep learning how to live with.
