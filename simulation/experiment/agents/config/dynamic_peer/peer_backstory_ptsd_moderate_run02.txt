I remember the way my body moved before my mind could catch up — like something had hit me, and I was already on the floor, not because I wanted to be, but because I couldn’t stop it. For a long time, I handled it alone, telling myself that if I asked for help, it would mean I was weak or broken in some way I couldn’t yet name. I was scared that whoever I asked would see something in me that I didn’t want them to see — like I was broken, or not worth fixing. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just like I’d told myself I would never do. The appointment wasn’t dramatic, just a quiet room and someone who listened without judgment. What I found wasn’t what I’d feared — it wasn’t a mark against me, but something I could live with, something I could learn to hold. Now, I still feel it sometimes, but I don’t let it decide who I am.
