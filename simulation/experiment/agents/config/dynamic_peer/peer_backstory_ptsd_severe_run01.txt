I was on the floor before I could stop myself, just like them — my body moving faster than my mind could keep up. For a long time, I handled it alone, telling myself that if I asked for help, it would mean I was broken or weak. I worried that if I talked to someone, they’d see something wrong with me, like I wasn’t really trying or I didn’t deserve to feel better. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just like I’d been avoiding doing for years. The appointment wasn’t dramatic — just sitting there, talking, listening. What I found out was that it didn’t mean I was broken, or that I had to be fixed. It just meant I was human, and that’s okay. I still have days when it catches me off guard, but now I know it doesn’t define me.
