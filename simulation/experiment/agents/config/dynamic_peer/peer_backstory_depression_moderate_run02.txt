There were times I didn’t show up, too — not because I didn’t want to, but because I thought showing up meant I had to be okay. I handled it alone for a while, convincing myself that if I asked for help, it would mean I was broken or weak. I was scared that someone would see me and think I was a burden, that I’d be judged for not being able to fix it on my own. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just like I’d always been told I should if things got too heavy. The first time I walked into that office, I felt like I was stepping into a room where I didn’t belong. But what happened instead was that someone listened, not because they had to, but because they wanted to. It wasn’t what I expected — it wasn’t a label or a diagnosis, just a quiet understanding that I wasn’t alone. Now, I still have days when I forget to show up, but I don’t pretend anymore. I just breathe, and wait for the next step.
