There were times I’d second-guess every small decision, like whether to say hello to a coworker or if I’d left the stove on. I didn’t want to admit I was struggling because I was afraid that if I asked for help, it would mean I was broken or weak. I worried that if I went to a counsellor, they’d think I was unstable or that there was something fundamentally wrong with me. Eventually, I went anyway — just a few sessions, sitting in a quiet room, talking about things that felt too big to hold on my own. What I found was that it wasn’t about being broken; it was about being human. Now, I still have days where I feel off-kilter, but I don’t let it define me the way I used to.
