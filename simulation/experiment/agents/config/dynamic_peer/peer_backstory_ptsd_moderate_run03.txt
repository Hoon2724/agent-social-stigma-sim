I used to be the one who always stayed quiet after things like that — the ones that happened too fast for me to stop. I handled it alone for a long time, because asking for help felt like admitting something was wrong with me. I was scared that if I told someone, they’d see me as weak, or broken, or not worth fixing. Eventually, I went to a counsellor, just like I’d told myself I never would. The first time I sat there, I didn’t say much, but I didn’t leave. What came up wasn’t what I expected — it wasn’t a label or a diagnosis, just the understanding that what happened wasn’t about me being flawed, but about how the world can feel too much sometimes. I still feel it, but now I know it doesn’t define me.
