There were times I’d skip plans and sit in my room for hours, not because I wanted to, but because the thought of showing up felt like a mistake I’d already made. I told myself I could handle it on my own — asking for anything felt like admitting I wasn’t okay, like there was something wrong with me that I couldn’t fix. I worried that if I went to a counsellor, they’d see me as broken or weak, like I’d failed in some way that made me untrustworthy. Eventually, I went, just once, and sat there quietly while the person listened. They didn’t say I was broken or broken-minded or anything like that — they just said, “this is hard, and it’s okay to feel this way.” It wasn’t a magic fix, but it was a start. Now, I still feel things deeply, but I know it doesn’t mean I’m flawed — just that I’m still learning how to carry it.
